# 💼 Business Email Compromise (BEC)

A **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** is a phishing attack that can target any organization, but it focuses on those that frequently transfer **large amounts of money** to purchase goods or pay vendors.  

Attackers monitor their targets over time to identify **trusted relationships** between companies and suppliers. Once identified, they either:  
- 📧 **Compromise an email account** of an executive or high-level employee.  
- 🎭 **Spoof the email address** to make it appear legitimate.  

The malicious actor then tricks employees into transferring funds to a **fraudulent bank account** under their control.

---

## 📌 Why It Works
- Emails appear **legitimate and convincing**.  
- Exploits **trust in business relationships**.  
- Uses **social engineering principles** to deceive recipients.  

---

## 📊 Impact
- In 2019, BEC attacks accounted for an estimated **$1.77 billion in losses in the USA**, according to the FBI.  
- Despite seeming “easy,” these attacks are **highly effective** when executed by experienced actors.  

---

## 🛡️ Defense Strategies
- 📚 **User awareness training**: Teach employees to verify requests before transferring funds.  
- 🔍 **Email authentication protocols** (SPF, DKIM, DMARC).  
- ✅ **Verification procedures** for financial transactions.  
- 🧩 **Separation of duties** to limit who can authorize payments.  

---

## ✨ Summary
BEC attacks exploit **trust, authority, and routine business processes**.  
Strong defenses require a combination of **education, technical safeguards, and strict internal policies**.
