# 📎 Email Attachments

---

## 📌 Overview
Email attachments are a common vector for **phishing and social engineering attacks**.  
Attackers use different tactics to trick recipients into opening files that may contain **malicious content** or links.  

---

## 🛠️ Types of Email Attachments

### 🔹 Social Engineering Files
- Attackers use **spoofed emails** by changing the domain or brand name.  
- Often combined with **impersonation tactics** to gain trust.  

---

### 🔹 Lure Documents
- Attachments that appear **non-malicious** (e.g., PDF, Word, Excel).  
- Contain **embedded hyperlinks** that redirect users to malicious websites.  
- Exploit curiosity or urgency to make the victim click.  

---

### 🔹 Malicious Files
- The most common phishing method.  
- Example: **MS Word documents with macros**.  
- When opened, macros execute and install malware on the victim’s system.  
- Can lead to **credential theft, ransomware, or persistence mechanisms**.  

---

## 🚨 Key Takeaway
Email attachments are one of the **primary delivery methods for malware**.  
Users must be trained to:
- Verify sender identity.  
- Avoid opening unexpected attachments.  
- Disable macros by default.  
- Report suspicious emails to the security team.  

---

## ✅ Conclusion
- **Social Engineering Files** → Spoofed domains + impersonation.  
- **Lure Documents** → Benign-looking files with malicious links.  
- **Malicious Files** → Executable content (e.g., macros) that installs malware.
