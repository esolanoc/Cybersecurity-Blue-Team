# 🛡️ Incident Response Plan

## 📌 Overview
Having an **Incident Response Plan (IRP)** can make all the difference when responding to a security incident.  
A well-documented plan ensures clarity, prevents confusion, and saves valuable time.  

IRPs are typically split into six sections:
- 🔧 Preparation  
- 🔍 Identification  
- 🛑 Containment  
- 🧹 Eradication  
- 🔄 Recovery  
- 📚 Lessons Learned  

---

## 🔧 Preparation
This is arguably the most important stage. It requires attention not only when writing the plan but also through **continuous training**.  

Key activities:
- 📝 Develop response plans for different incident types and run simulated scenarios.  
- 💻 Ensure resources are ready: laptops, forensic tools, software, and training.  
- 👥 Train and evaluate team members regularly to ensure readiness.  

---

## 🔍 Identification
Focuses on the ability to **detect and analyze incidents**.  

Questions to answer:
- When did the incident occur?  
- Who discovered it and how?  
- What systems or business units are affected?  
- What is the scope and impact?  

Organizations may assign:
- ⚡ **Criticality level** → How fast must we respond?  
- 📊 **Impact level** → How long will operations be affected?  

---

## 🛑 Containment
Goal: **Stop the spread** of the incident.  

Actions:
- Disconnect compromised devices.  
- Preserve digital evidence carefully.  
- Use backups to maintain business continuity.  

---

## 🧹 Eradication
Analyze and remove malicious artifacts.  

Methods:
- Use MITRE ATT&CK framework for analysis.  
- Review logs, packet captures, and SIEM data.  
- Remove malware, persistence mechanisms, and harden systems.  

---

## 🔄 Recovery
Return systems to production safely.  

Example:  
- Use backup servers temporarily.  
- Clean and harden compromised systems before reintroducing them.  

---

## 📚 Lessons Learned
After resolution, hold a **post-incident meeting**:  
- Recap what happened.  
- Identify strengths and weaknesses.  
- Update documentation, policies, and request resources if needed.  

---

## ✅ Conclusion
Incidents are tough, but they provide opportunities to **improve defenses**.  
Regular updates, training, and simulated exercises keep the team ready.
